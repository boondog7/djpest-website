Why Girrawheen gets the pests it gets

Girrawheen was rezoned for public housing in 1969 as part of the satellite city planned around Mirrabooka, and most of the suburb was built through the 1970s. The typical home is single-storey brick and tile with timber roof framing on a compact lot, alongside grouped units and, increasingly, duplex and triplex infill as original blocks are subdivided. The ground is the transition zone between Perth's dune systems: Spearwood yellow sand toward Wanneroo Road giving way to grey Bassendean sand as you head east toward Koondoola. Fifty-year-old timber framing, timber fences and old retic mean termite risk is real, and dense housing means a cockroach or rodent problem in one unit rarely stays in one unit.

The suburb is bounded by Wanneroo Road, Beach Road, Marangaroo Drive and Mirrabooka Avenue, and it is generous with open space: Hainsworth Park on Salcott Road, Blackmore Park on Blackmore Avenue and Montrose Park all carry mature trees and long turf edges. Directly across Marangaroo Drive is Koondoola Regional Bushland, a large banksia and jarrah woodland on Bassendean sand that supports goannas, bandicoot-sized wildlife and a healthy rodent population. Homes along the northern streets get the bushland spillover of rats, native cockroaches and spiders. We treat accordingly: proofing and secured baiting on the bush side, gel baiting for German cockroaches in shared kitchens, and a low-odour non-staining internal treatment suited to grouped housing.

What we treat most in Girrawheen

The Girrawheen pest short-list.

Termites

1970s timber roof framing, timber fences and pergolas, and subdivided blocks where the new slab sits hard against old landscaping. Inspections to AS 4349.3 and management systems to AS 3660.2. Termite inspection.

Coastal brown ants

Super-colonies under the paving, driveways and shared courtyards of grouped housing, moving between units. Non-repellent products and baits, APVMA-registered and applied to their label, treated across the whole property rather than one flat at a time. Ant management.

Rats and mice

Koondoola bushland to the north, big parks in the middle and older tiled roofs throughout. Roof rats along the Marangaroo Drive side, mice in unit blocks. Proofing first, then secured bait stations. Rodent management.

German cockroaches

Grouped units and shared walls let German cockroaches move between kitchens. A gel-bait and dust programme applied to label, with a follow-up visit, is what actually clears them. Cockroach management.

Are 1970s Girrawheen homes at termite risk?

Yes, and it is often overlooked. These homes have timber roof framing, timber door frames and usually a timber pergola or fence touching the house somewhere. Original barriers, where they existed, are long gone. An inspection to AS 4349.3 every twelve months is the standard, and a management system to AS 3660.2 where activity is found. Book an inspection.

The ants come from the units next door. Can you still treat them?

Yes. Coastal brown ants form super-colonies that span several properties, so the treatment has to be colony-level: a slow-acting non-repellent product or bait carried back to the queens rather than a repellent spray that only pushes them next door and back again. Where a strata or landlord agrees, treating the whole group at once works far better. Read more.

Do you treat rentals and public housing in Girrawheen?

Yes. We work with tenants, private landlords, property managers and strata, and we document every area treated so the manager has a clear record. Internal treatments are low-odour and non-staining, and we tell tenants exactly what to do before and after the visit.
