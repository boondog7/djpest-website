## Rats, mice, and why winter is the season.
Three species cover almost every rodent call in Perth's northern suburbs. Black rats (Rattus rattus) are climbers: they enter roof voids along gutter lines, eaves and unsealed roof penetrations, and they are the usual reason for the scratching above the bedroom at 2 am. Norway rats (Rattus norvegicus) are larger, ground-based and prefer sub-floors, drains and burrows under sheds. House mice (Mus musculus) need a gap the width of a pen and a little dropped food.
- Scratching, scurrying or gnawing in the ceiling, usually from dusk.
- Droppings in the roof void, garage, pantry, under the sink or along the fence line.
- Gnaw marks on cables, sarking, pet-food bags and fruit on the tree.
- Grease rubs along a wall or beam where a rat runs the same route each night.
- A pet suddenly fixated on one corner of the kitchen or the ceiling.
Rodents do not come inside for shelter alone. They come for food and water. A dripping tap, an open compost bin, fallen citrus or a bird feeder does more to keep them than any gap in the eaves. We look at why before we treat what.How we treat it

## Rodent control FAQ.
Q: How do I tell if I have rats or mice?
Rats leave droppings 8 to 12 mm long, gnaw holes the size of a 50-cent coin and thump across the roof void at night. Mice leave rice-grain droppings of 3 to 6 mm, gnaw smaller holes and scurry. Black rats, the usual northern-suburbs roof invader, are climbers; Norway rats are ground and sub-floor animals. Treatment differs, so we identify first.
Q: How long until the rats are gone?
Most domestic jobs settle within two to three weeks. Rats are neophobic: they avoid new objects in their territory for several days, so a station that looks untouched at day five is normal, not a failure. Mice usually feed within a day or two. The follow-up visit checks consumption and repositions stations along the runs.
Q: Is baiting a risk to pets and kids?
Baits go only in locked, tamper-resistant stations anchored in place and positioned where children and pets cannot reach the block. Inside the house, where pets or kids are present, we prefer snap traps in covered boxes to avoid a pet catching a poisoned rodent. Every placement is listed on your report and we brief you on what to watch for.
Q: Will they come back?
Almost every re-infestation is an unsealed entry point. A rat needs a gap about the size of a 20-cent coin; a mouse gets through a gap the width of a pen. We map every gap we find and give you a sealing plan, do the simple seals on the day, and quote anything larger. Rodent baiting carries a three-month re-treatment promise.
Q: Do you go into the roof void?
Yes, where the manhole and clearance make it safe. Most northern-suburbs rat activity is black rats in the roof, entering at gutter joints, eave gaps and unsealed roof penetrations. We inspect the void, place stations or traps there, then seal externally.
Q: What does rodent control cost in Perth?
A rodent program with the initial visit, two tamper-resistant stations and a written proofing plan typically runs $220–$380. Follow-up checks are $90–$140. Additional stations, roof or sub-floor access and sealing work are itemised on the quote.
Q: Why do rodents come inside in winter?
Warmth and food. Perth's May to August rains push rats and mice out of bush margins and garden beds toward roof voids and garages, and a dripping tap or a pet bowl left out overnight keeps them there. Sealing entry points before winter beats baiting in July.

## Why the accountant's approach matters

What the rules ask of you
## Documented, or it did not happen.
Food businesses. Standard 3.2.2 of the Food Standards Code, applied in WA through the Food Act 2008, requires you to take all practicable measures to prevent pests entering and to eradicate and prevent their harbourage. Your local government's environmental health officers inspect against it. Droppings behind the fryer, a German cockroach in the coffee machine or no evidence of a program are the fastest routes to a poor result, a follow-up inspection and, in the worst case, a public notice. HACCP-based food safety programs and third-party audits go further: they want to see the station map, the service history, safety data sheets and licences in a folder, and a trend they can read.
Strata. The council of owners is responsible for common property. Cockroaches travel between lots through service risers and bin chutes, rodents run the basement and the bin room, and a program that only treats the lot that complained is a program that keeps complaining. Strata managers need a report they can table.
Childcare, aged care and health. Sensitive people, strict re-entry periods, product choices that suit the site and servicing when rooms are empty. Regulators and parents both ask what was used and when; the record answers.
Warehouses and workshops. Rodents are the usual issue: pallets, cardboard, roller doors that do not seal and food in the lunchroom. Numbered external and internal bait stations, proofing that actually gets done, and stock protected.
Offices and retail. Lower risk, quarterly is usually enough, but the kitchenette and the loading dock still need looking at, and a tenant's lease often requires a program in place.How a program runs
