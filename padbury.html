Why Padbury gets the pests it gets

Padbury was one of the four Whitfords suburbs rezoned from coastal land in 1969 and named in 1971, so the original housing is early-1970s double brick and brick veneer with tiled roofs on timber framing, modest homes on large blocks. Rezoning has since brought battle-axe subdivisions and new two-storey builds beside 50-year-old originals, which means a street can have old timber framing and fresh slab penetrations side by side, and a new home with a termite management system next to an old one without. The ground is Spearwood dune sand over Tamala limestone, which sits close to the surface on the higher ground and gives termites the moisture pockets they need through summer.

The south-east corner is the Hepburn Heights bushland, 38 hectares of remnant banksia and tuart woodland saved from development in the 1990s, alongside Pinnaroo Valley Memorial Park and the water reservoir. That block of bush is the source of the rats, native cockroaches, huntsmen and paper wasps that the surrounding streets deal with each autumn. Whitfords Avenue and Hepburn Avenue bound the suburb, the Mitchell Freeway and Whitfords station sit on the eastern edge, and the ovals at McDonald Park and the school grounds keep rodents moving. Older Padbury homes get an annual termite inspection; newer subdivisions get a check that the management system was not bridged by landscaping.

What we treat most in Padbury

The Padbury pest short-list.

Termites

Fifty-year-old timber roof framing next to new builds whose barriers get bridged by paving and garden beds. Inspections to AS 4349.3 and management systems to AS 3660.2, drilled and injected where the limestone is shallow. Termite inspection.

Coastal brown ants

Warm sand over limestone is their habitat, and Padbury's paving, retaining walls and pool surrounds host linked super-colonies. Slow-acting non-repellent products and baits, APVMA-registered, applied to label. Ant treatment.

Rats in the roof

Hepburn Heights bush and the memorial park supply them; old tiled roofs with gaps at the eaves and lifted flashings invite them. Baiting in stations plus proofing. Rodent management.

Spiders and cockroaches

Redbacks in retic boxes and under coping, black house spiders on 1970s brickwork, native and American cockroaches from the bush edge and old stormwater lines. General pest treatment.

How long does it take you to reach Padbury?

About ten minutes from our Warwick base, up the Mitchell Freeway to Hepburn or Whitfords Avenue. Same-day for active pests such as a wasp nest, ants indoors or rats in the roof is usually possible, and routine treatments are booked at a time that suits you and confirmed in writing.

Are original 1970s Padbury homes a termite risk?

They are the highest-risk housing we service. Fifty-year-old timber roof framing, timber fences and pergolas on free-draining sand over limestone, often with garden beds built up over the weep holes. Most were built with no termite management system at all. A yearly inspection to AS 4349.3 is the minimum. Book a termite inspection.

What works on the ants in Padbury?

Coastal brown ants are the main problem and they need colony-level treatment. Their super-colonies have many queens across several properties, so a surface spray only splits them. We use slow-acting non-repellent products and gel or granular baits, APVMA-registered and applied to label, that the workers carry back. Read more.

We are in a new subdivision in Padbury. Does that change anything?

Yes. New homes are built with a termite management system to AS 3660.2, but paving, garden beds and retic that go in later can bridge it, and the older neighbouring homes carry established termite pressure. We check the slab edge and penetrations and confirm the system has not been compromised. Termite inspection.
