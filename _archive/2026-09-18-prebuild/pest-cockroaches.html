Cockroaches in Perth homes

German and Australian cockroaches behave very differently — and the right response depends on knowing which one you have. Here is how to tell them apart, where they hide in Perth kitchens and subfloors, the signs that give them away, and the housekeeping that helps keep them out.

01 — Identification

Know which roach you're looking at

Most Perth callouts involve one of two cockroaches. The small German cockroach is an indoor breeder that thrives in warm, humid kitchens. The larger Australian cockroach tends to come in from the garden, subfloor or roof void. Telling them apart matters — it changes where we look and how we treat.

German cockroach

Small, fast, indoor

Blattella germanica

  • Size Roughly 12–15 mm — small enough to hide in a hinge or appliance seam
  • Colour Light tan to brown, with two dark parallel stripes behind the head
  • Habit Breeds indoors; prefers warm, humid kitchens and bathrooms
  • Tell Seen in daylight usually means higher numbers nearby
Australian cockroach

Large, glossy, from outside

Periplaneta australasiae

  • Size Roughly 30–35 mm — a much larger, heavier-bodied insect
  • Colour Reddish-brown and glossy, with pale yellow edges to the wing bases
  • Habit Lives outdoors in gardens, subfloors and roof voids; wanders inside
  • Tell Usually a few large individuals rather than a swarm of small ones
General guidance only. Appearance varies with age and lighting, and other species turn up in Perth too. This page is indicative and is not a substitute for professional on-site identification or medical advice. In a medical emergency, call 000.
02 — Where they live

The warm, hidden, damp places

Cockroaches want three things — warmth, moisture and somewhere dark to wedge into. In Perth homes that points to a short list of spots, and it's where we focus an inspection.

Kitchens

Behind and under the fridge, near the dishwasher and kettle, inside cupboard corners and the warm motor housings of appliances. Grease and crumbs make a kitchen ideal.

Subfloors

The cool, sheltered cavity beneath a suspended floor holds moisture and clutter. Larger outdoor species commonly harbour here before moving up into the home.

Wall voids & cavities

The hollow spaces inside walls, around pipe and cable runs and behind kick-boards give roaches a protected highway between rooms, well out of sight.

Drains & wet areas

Floor wastes, laundry and bathroom drains, leaking traps and the damp under-sink cupboard. Standing water and a drink source keep a population going.

03 — Signs of activity

What to look for before you ever see one

Cockroaches are mostly nocturnal, so you'll often notice the evidence before the insect. Four signs are worth checking, especially in and around the kitchen.

01

Droppings

Tiny dark specks like ground pepper from small roaches, or larger pellet-shaped droppings from big ones, gathering in cupboard corners and along edges.

02

Smear marks

Irregular dark-brown smears and streaks in moist, heavily used runways where roaches travel repeatedly along surfaces.

03

Egg cases

Small brown capsule-shaped cases tucked into cracks and behind appliances. Finding them points to breeding on site, not just a wanderer.

04

Odour

A distinctive musty, oily smell that grows stronger as numbers build — often the first thing people notice in a cupboard or small room.

04 — Why it matters

More a hygiene and allergen issue than a hazard

Cockroaches move between drains, bins and food-preparation surfaces, so they can carry bacteria from one to the other and contaminate food and benchtops. That makes them primarily a food-hygiene concern in the home.

They are also a recognised indoor allergen. Their droppings, shed skins and body fragments can become part of household dust, and for some sensitive people this has been associated with asthma and allergy symptoms. The effect varies a great deal from person to person.

None of this needs to be dramatised. The sensible response is to reduce numbers, cut off their food and water, and have an established population identified and treated properly rather than left to grow. For any health concern, speak to your doctor — and in a medical emergency, call 000.

In short
  • They travel between drains, bins and benchtops, so they can spread bacteria onto food surfaces.
  • Droppings and shed skins are a recognised indoor allergen that has been linked to asthma in sensitive people.
  • Seeing them during the day often signals higher numbers hidden nearby.
  • A breeding population indoors rarely clears on its own — it's worth acting early.
05 — Prevention

What you can safely do yourself

Good housekeeping is the single most effective thing a homeowner can do. The aim is simple — take away the food, water and shelter that cockroaches depend on. None of this involves any products; leave treatment to a licensed technician.

Clean up food and grease

Wipe benchtops and the stovetop after cooking, sweep up crumbs, clean behind and under appliances where grease builds, and store dry food in sealed containers.

Fix moisture

Repair dripping taps and leaking traps, dry out the under-sink cupboard, ventilate steamy bathrooms and laundries, and don't leave water sitting in sinks overnight.

Seal cracks and gaps

Close up gaps around pipes, skirting, kick-boards and cupboards, and fit door seals. Fewer harbourage points and entry routes means fewer places to settle.

Manage bins and clutter

Use bins with tight lids, empty kitchen waste regularly, keep outdoor bins away from doors, and clear cardboard and clutter that gives roaches somewhere to hide.

Prevention only. The steps above are non-chemical housekeeping you can do yourself. We do not recommend or describe any do-it-yourself products or treatments on this site. If you have an active infestation, the effective step is to have it identified and treated by our licensed technicians.
06 — Questions

Cockroach questions, answered

How do I know if it's German or Australian cockroaches?
Size is the quickest clue. German cockroaches are small, light tan and carry two dark stripes behind the head, and they breed indoors. Australian cockroaches are much larger, glossy reddish-brown with pale wing edges, and usually come in from the garden, subfloor or roof void. When we visit, part of the job is confirming the species on site, because it changes where we inspect.
I've only seen one — do I really have a problem?
Not necessarily, but it's worth a look. A single large cockroach can simply be a wanderer that came in from outside. Seeing small cockroaches in daylight, or finding droppings, smear marks or egg cases, is more concerning because it suggests numbers building out of sight. If you're unsure, a quick call lets us talk it through.
Why do I have cockroaches when my home is clean?
Cleanliness helps a lot, but it isn't the whole story. Cockroaches follow warmth and moisture, and they can arrive in deliveries, cardboard boxes, second-hand appliances or through shared walls and drains in units. Even a tidy kitchen can offer enough behind a warm fridge motor. Housekeeping reduces the risk; it doesn't always rule it out.
Are cockroaches a health risk to my family?
They are best thought of as a hygiene and allergen issue rather than an immediate hazard. Because they move between drains, bins and benchtops they can carry bacteria onto food surfaces, and their droppings and shed skins are a recognised indoor allergen that can affect sensitive people. The practical answer is to reduce numbers and keep food and water out of reach. This is general information, not medical advice — if you have a health concern, speak to your doctor, and in a medical emergency call 000.
