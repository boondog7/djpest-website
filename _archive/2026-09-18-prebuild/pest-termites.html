Subterranean termites in Perth

Australia's most destructive timber pest works quietly, underground and out of sight. On Perth's sandy soils — especially near bushland and reticulation — a colony can reach a home's structural timbers long before anyone sees a single insect. Here is how to recognise the signs, what you can safely do yourself, and when to book a proper inspection.

01 — Identification

What subterranean termites actually look like

The everyday workers

The termites that cause damage are the soft-bodied workers — and most people never see them, because they avoid light and stay sealed inside timber or mud galleries. They are commonly mistaken for "white ants", but termites are not ants at all.

  • Pale and soft — creamy-white, straight-sided body roughly the size of a grain of rice.
  • No pinched waist — the body is even and sausage-shaped, unlike an ant's narrow waist.
  • Straight, beaded antennae — not elbowed like an ant's.
  • Light-shy — they retreat instantly when timber or a mud lead is opened.

Termite or flying ant?

The version you are most likely to notice is the winged reproductive — the swarmer, or "alate" — usually on a warm, humid evening. Telling them apart from flying ants is the key to an early call.

  • Four equal wings — termite wings are all the same length; flying ants have a larger front pair.
  • Even, straight body — no obvious waist.
  • Straight antennae — ants have bent (elbowed) antennae.
  • Shed wings — termite wings drop off in neat piles soon after they land.

Several termite species live across the Perth metro area, and accurate species identification genuinely matters — it changes how a property should be inspected and managed. That part is a job for an on-site licensed technician, not a photo.

02 — Around Perth homes

Where they turn up across the northern suburbs

Subterranean termites nest in the soil and travel from there to a food source — your home's timber. Perth's environment suits them well, and a few common settings raise the odds for homes in Joondalup, Hillarys, Duncraig, Kingsley, Warwick, Wanneroo, Currambine, Mindarie, Sorrento, Greenwood and Padbury.

  • Sandy, free-draining soils — typical of the coastal plain and easy for colonies to move through.
  • Properties near bushland or reserves — natural nesting habitat sits close to the back fence.
  • Reticulation and damp ground — leaking sprinklers, garden beds against the house and poor drainage create the moisture termites seek.
  • Older timber-framed homes — and any property with stored timber, stumps, retaining sleepers or untreated decking close to the structure.
  • Concealed entry points — gaps in slabs, weep holes, and where soil or mulch is heaped above the floor level.
03 — Signs of activity

The warning signs worth acting on

Because termites stay hidden, the evidence usually shows up before the insects do. If you spot any of these, stop and book an inspection.

Mud leads & tubes

Pencil-thin tunnels of dried mud running up walls, piers, slab edges or across brickwork — the termites' protected highways from soil to timber.

Hollow-sounding timber

Skirtings, architraves, door frames or floors that sound hollow or papery when tapped, because the timber is being eaten out from the inside.

Discarded wings

Small piles of identical shed wings near windowsills, light fittings or webs after a warm, humid evening — left behind once swarmers land.

Bubbling or rippled paint

Blistered, cracked or sunken paint and plaster — often where activity sits just behind the surface, sometimes with a thin earthy crust underneath.

Sticking doors & windows

Frames that suddenly jam or warp can reflect moisture and movement in the timber that termites are working through.

Live termites uncovered

Pale, soft insects exposed when a piece of timber, skirting or garden sleeper is disturbed. Reseal it and call — don't disturb them further.

Important: if you find what looks like live termites, leave the area undisturbed and avoid poking at it or spraying anything from the hardware store — disturbing a working point can push the colony elsewhere and make professional inspection harder. The right next step is a phone call, not a do-it-yourself fix.
04 — Why it matters

Quiet damage, real cost

For most Australian households the home is the single largest asset, and subterranean termites are widely recognised as the country's most economically significant timber pest. Their damage to structural timber is commonly reported to fall outside standard building insurance, which is why regular inspection and sensible prevention matter so much. [general claim — see sources]

Because the workers feed from the inside out and keep the outer surface intact, damage can build up unseen over months or years. By the time the signs are obvious on the surface, the timber behind them may already be compromised.

None of this is cause for panic. Termites work slowly, and an active infestation found early and managed properly is a very different situation to one left unchecked. The most reliable protection is a routine professional inspection on a sensible schedule — and acting the moment something looks off.

05 — Prevention you can do

Steps you can safely take to make your home less inviting

These are housekeeping, common-sense measures. They reduce the moisture and easy food termites look for — they do not replace a professional barrier or inspection.

01

Control moisture

Fix dripping taps, hot-water overflows and air-conditioner runoff. Termites are drawn to consistently damp ground around a home.

02

Check reticulation

Direct sprinklers away from the building and repair leaking reticulation so the soil against the slab isn't kept wet.

03

Keep timber off the ground

Move firewood, offcuts, sleepers and stored timber away from walls and up off the soil — don't stack it against the house.

04

Pull mulch back

Keep garden beds, mulch and soil below the slab edge and clear of weep holes, so any activity can't reach timber unseen.

05

Ventilate subfloors

Keep subfloor vents clear and unobstructed so the space underneath stays dry and well aired.

06

Keep inspection access clear

Leave room around the perimeter, subfloor and roof void so a technician can actually see and reach the at-risk areas.

07

Trim back vegetation

Cut back shrubs and trees touching the house and remove old stumps and roots near the structure where colonies settle.

08

Book regular inspections

The most reliable prevention of all — a professional inspection on a routine schedule catches activity while it's still minor.

Please note: prevention here means housekeeping only. We never recommend that homeowners apply any product, bait or treatment themselves. Any treatment is carried out by our licensed technicians using APVMA-registered products, strictly to label, after an on-site inspection.
06 — Common questions

Termite questions we hear most

Are "white ants" the same thing as termites?
Yes — "white ants" is the old common name Perth locals use, but they aren't ants at all. They're subterranean termites, and the everyday name doesn't change how seriously they should be taken.
I found wings on the windowsill but no insects. Should I worry?
Discarded wings are a classic early sign that swarmers have landed nearby looking to start a new colony. It doesn't confirm activity inside your home, but it's a strong reason to have an inspection done rather than wait and see.
How often should a Perth home be inspected?
A routine professional inspection to AS 4349.3 is the standard approach, with the interval set to your property's risk and history. Higher-risk sites — near bushland, with damp ground or past activity — generally warrant more frequent checks. We'll advise a sensible schedule for your home after we've seen it.
Can I just spray them myself if I find some?
We strongly advise against it. Disturbing or spraying a working point can scatter the colony and make it harder to locate and manage properly. Leave the area undisturbed, reseal anything you opened, and call us — termite management is licensed work for good reason.
What does a termite treatment involve?
It starts with an on-site inspection to confirm the situation, then a management plan suited to your property and carried out to AS 3660.2 by a licensed technician using APVMA-registered products, strictly to label. Eligible treatments come with our written 8-year warranty. We'll walk you through exactly what's proposed before any work begins.
I think I have active termites right now. What do I do?
