Identification

How to recognise a redback

The female is the one most people picture. Males look so different that many homeowners assume they belong to another species entirely.

Female · the familiar one

Glossy black with a red back stripe

A rounded, pea-sized to roughly fingernail-sized body, deep black and shiny, with a distinctive red to orange stripe running along the top of the rounded abdomen. Many also show a reddish hourglass-shaped marking on the underside. Long, slender legs.

  • Body deep, glossy black
  • Red or orange stripe along the upper abdomen
  • Often a red hourglass mark underneath
  • Rounded, bulbous abdomen on thin legs
  • Builds a messy, strong-threaded web with a funnel-like retreat
Male · easily missed

Much smaller and lighter in colour

Males are a fraction of the female's size — small enough that they are often overlooked. They tend to be lighter, brownish, and may carry faint light markings rather than the bold red stripe. Because they are so small, the female and her web are usually what give a redback away.

  • Far smaller than the female
  • Lighter, often brownish body
  • Markings faint or pale rather than vivid red
  • Frequently goes unnoticed near a female's web
  • Spiderlings start pale and darken as they mature
Indicative only. Several Perth spiders can look similar at a glance, and lighting, age and angle change how markings appear. This is a guide to recognising a redback, not a substitute for professional on-site identification. If you are unsure what you have found, leave it alone and book an inspection.
Where they harbour

The dry, sheltered spots around a Perth home

Redbacks favour still, dry, undisturbed spaces out of wind and rain — usually closer to the house than people expect.

Sheds & garages

Quiet, dry and rarely disturbed — corners, behind stored items, and along the gaps where wall meets floor are classic redback territory.

Under outdoor furniture

The undersides of tables, chairs, benches and BBQ trolleys offer the still, shaded space they prefer — right where hands and legs go.

Eaves & roof voids

Sheltered overhangs, eaves and the edges of roof spaces stay dry and undisturbed, which suits a spider that dislikes wind and rain.

Retaining walls

The cavities, weep holes and gaps in brick and block retaining walls are a favourite — warm, dark, and out of the weather.

Letterboxes

One of the most common surprises. A quiet, dry, dark box that people reach into daily is exactly the kind of harbourage a redback likes.

Pots, clutter & toys

Stacked pots, stored timber, children's outdoor toys and general clutter all create the still, sheltered pockets redbacks settle into.

Signs of activity

What gives a redback away

You will usually spot the web and the female long before you see a male. The signs are subtle, but consistent once you know what to look for.

  • 01

    A messy, strong web

    An untidy tangle of unusually tough silk, often with a funnel-shaped retreat tucked into a corner or cavity.

  • 02

    Web debris below

    Bits of leaf litter, dead insects and small prey caught and held in the lower threads.

  • 03

    Round, pale egg sacs

    Several creamy, ball-shaped egg sacs suspended in the web are a strong sign of an established female.

  • 04

    Repeat sightings in one spot

    Finding spiders or webs again and again in the same sheltered area usually means a settled population, not a one-off.

Medical significance

Take a redback bite seriously — and get the right advice fast

A redback bite can be medically significant, and reactions vary from person to person. Pain, sweating and feeling generally unwell are commonly reported, and symptoms can develop over time. The right response is simple: do not wait it out, and seek proper medical advice.

Prevention you can do

Make your home a poor fit for redbacks

Sensible, hands-off housekeeping that removes the dark, still spaces redbacks rely on. No products, no DIY treatments — those are our job.

Declutter the yard

Clear stacked pots, stored timber, bricks and unused items. Fewer hidden pockets means far fewer places a redback can settle.

Wear gloves in dark spaces

Reaching blindly into sheds, under benches or into the letterbox is how most bites happen. Gloves and a quick look first make a real difference.

Clear away webbing

Brush down webs from eaves, corners, fences and furniture regularly. Removing the web removes the harbourage a female depends on.

Check under furniture

Tip and inspect outdoor chairs, tables and BBQ trolleys before use — especially after they have sat unused over winter.

Rethink outdoor lighting

Lights draw the insects redbacks prey on. Cooler-toned or movement-sensor lighting near doors reduces the food supply they gather around.

Tidy gaps and cavities

Keep retaining-wall cavities, meter boxes and letterboxes tidy and closed up where practical, so there are fewer sheltered pockets to settle into.

Prevention only. These are safe, non-chemical steps a homeowner can take. We do not provide do-it-yourself treatment, mixing or application advice — controlling an active redback population around your home is work for a licensed technician.
Common questions

Redback questions, answered plainly

I found a redback near my back door — what should I do?
Keep children and pets away from the area and do not try to handle it. If anyone has been bitten and is unwell, use the medical contacts above — call 000 in an emergency. For the spider itself, give us a call and we will arrange a visit. Our licensed technicians treat redbacks; we do not recommend you apply anything yourself.
Why is the redback your logo?
It is the spider Perth households recognise instantly, and it represents exactly what we do — finding the things that hide in the quiet corners of a home and dealing with them properly. The white spider and red hourglass on our badge is a nod to taking that work seriously.
How long after treatment am I covered?
Our spider treatments come with 90-day coverage. If redbacks return in that window, we come back. Coverage details are set out in writing with your service so there are no surprises.
Do redbacks come back after treatment?
Redbacks live where conditions suit them, so pressure from neighbouring yards and sheltered harbourage can bring new spiders in over time. That is why we pair treatment with practical prevention advice, and why the 90-day coverage exists — to keep things in check if activity returns soon after a visit.
Are redbacks worse at certain times of year?
Sightings often pick up in the warmer months when spiders are most active and people are using outdoor areas, sheds and furniture again. They can be found year-round in Perth, though, because they favour dry, sheltered spots that stay comfortable through winter.
