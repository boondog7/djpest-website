The spiders that actually matter in Perth.

Australia has a reputation. Most of it is undeserved. Of the dozens of spider species you'll encounter in a Perth garden, only two are genuinely worth your concern medically — the redback (Latrodectus hasselti) and arguably the white-tail (Lampona cylindrata/murina) — and the white-tail's "necrotic ulcer" reputation has been thoroughly debunked by Australian clinical research. The huntsman that scares the absolute hell out of you when it sprints across the wall? Mild bite, no medical significance. The wolf spider? Same. Daddy-long-legs? Eats other spiders, you should welcome them.

Redbacks live outdoors in dry sheltered spots — under coping (the lip of pavers, retaining wall caps, pool coping), inside reticulation boxes (the buried valve boxes for your sprinkler system — peak hiding spot), inside garden sheds (especially under stored items), behind outdoor furniture, and in roller-door rails. They almost never enter the home. The "redback in the laundry" cliché is real but rare — usually they hitchhike inside on something brought in from the shed.

How we treat spiders (properly).

1. Long-residual perimeter — the harbourage zones

Spiders pick up residual chemical on their legs as they walk treated surfaces, and self-groom it onto their mouthparts. We target the harbourage zones first: eaves and ceiling cavities, weep holes (where redbacks shelter), fence lines and sub-floor vents, retic valve boxes (with care — we don't spray inside the box, we treat the perimeter and lid), garage corners, and under-coping zones on patios and pool surrounds.

2. Web removal first, treatment second

A treated surface only matters if the spider walks across it. Existing webs need physical removal (broom, telescopic web brush) before treatment so spiders re-establish on the freshly treated surface, not on top of old web silk. We do this as part of every job.

3. Reticulation box check — the redback hotspot

The single highest-yield redback hiding spot on most Perth properties is the buried sprinkler valve box. We open every visible retic box on the property, photo any redback we find, brief you, and treat the lid and perimeter. Don't reach into them blind — that's the most common Perth redback-bite scenario.

4. Internal treatment for white-tails (where appropriate)

White-tails are the indoor concern. Treatment is targeted spray along skirting boards, behind wardrobes, and in roof void margins. Limited residual indoor — we use the lowest-impact product compatible with the result.

Frequently asked about spiders.

Are redbacks deadly in Perth?

Redback bites are dangerous, but deaths have been extremely rare since the antivenom was introduced in 1956. Australia hasn't recorded a redback fatality in decades. That said, a redback bite is a medical emergency — symptoms include severe localised pain, sweating, nausea, and muscle spasms. If bitten: apply ice, do not bandage, get to an emergency department immediately. Children, elderly, and immunocompromised are highest risk.

Where do redbacks hide in Perth homes?

Under coping (the underside of pavers, retaining wall caps, pool coping), inside reticulation boxes (the buried valve boxes for your sprinkler system), inside garden sheds (especially under stored items), behind outdoor furniture, in roller-door cavity rails, and under the rim of unused outdoor pots. They love dry, sheltered, undisturbed spaces. They almost never come inside the house — they're outdoor dwellers.

What about white-tail spiders?

White-tails (Lampona cylindrata, L. murina) are nomadic indoor wanderers, particularly active in summer evenings. They don't build webs — they hunt other spiders. Bites can cause local pain and minor skin irritation, but the long-circulated "white-tail spiders cause necrotic skin ulcers" claim has been thoroughly debunked by Australian medical research. Bites are not pleasant but they're not destructive.

Will treatment kill all the spiders forever?

No, and beware anyone who claims it will. We treat surfaces with a long-residual chemical that affects spiders that walk through it (they pick it up on legs, transfer to mouthparts during grooming). Spiders that drop into your yard from neighbouring properties post-treatment will be affected as long as residual is active — typically 3 months. Annual treatment for properties with high pressure, otherwise on-call as needed.

Are huntsman spiders dangerous?

No. Huntsman are large and visually alarming but not medically significant. Bites are mild, comparable to a bee sting. They eat cockroaches and other pests — they're net beneficial in your roof void or shed. We don't recommend specifically targeting huntsman unless they're inside the home regularly. Most spider treatments will affect them as collateral but specifically hunting them is unnecessary.

Is spider treatment safe around pets and kids?

Yes when applied properly. Long-residual spray is applied to surfaces only (eaves, weep holes, fence lines, sub-floor) — re-entry time of approximately 2 hours for external work. Pets shouldn't lick treated surfaces during the re-entry window. We brief you on placements and re-entry times before we start.

When's the best time to treat spiders in Perth?

Late spring and early summer (October-December) is peak spider activity in Perth. That's when you'll see the most webs, the most males roaming for mates, and the most redback nest establishment. A treatment in October-November sets the property up for the high-pressure summer period. Annual is enough for most domestic properties.
