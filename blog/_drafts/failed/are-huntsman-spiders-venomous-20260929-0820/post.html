Are huntsman spiders venomous?

Yes, huntsman spiders (family Sparassidae) have venom. No, it's not dangerous to humans. The venom is specialised for their prey (insects) and delivered through fangs too small and weak to penetrate human skin effectively. A bite from a huntsman is so rare that most people who find one in their home will never experience one.

When threatened, huntsman spiders run first. They're fast and they flee. If trapped or handled roughly, they can bite, but the venom produces only mild discomfort, a slight sting or redness like an insect bite. No systemic effects, no medical emergency. The Australian Museum confirms that huntsman pose no medical risk to people.

What huntsman spiders look like

Huntsman are large, flat and unmistakably fast. The body grows to around 15 mm, but their legs spread across 100 to 150 mm, that's why they look so alarming. Their colour is usually pale brown, olive green, or mottled grey. You'll see them called "giant crab spiders" or "tarantulas" in Australia, both local names for the Sparassidae family.

The flat body is their signature. It lets them squeeze into tight spaces: under eaves, inside wall cavities, behind curtains, under furniture. Look for them on walls and ceilings, especially at night when they're hunting the insects attracted to your lights.

Where huntsman spiders hide in Perth homes

Huntsman prefer outdoors but often shelter inside during spring and summer. In homes with a roof void, they hide in the void itself or behind gutters. In brick veneer homes, they access the cavity between bricks and interior walls. In raised timber homes with a sub-floor, they move through the sub-floor space hunting prey insects. Even in slab-on-ground homes, they cluster around the roof void and exterior walls.

Inside the house, they gather near windows and lights because flying insects concentrate there. You'll find them behind curtains, in sheds and garages, in wardrobes, and under furniture. They don't build webs; they hunt actively, moving at night.

Why huntsman spiders are active in spring

Spring in Perth (September to November) brings warmer weather and more flying insects. Huntsman follow their food source. As temperatures climb, insects emerge from winter dormancy, and huntsman move out of their hiding places to hunt. October through March is peak season for spotting them in homes.

Huntsman are beneficial predators. They eat cockroaches, flies, moths and other pest insects. If you leave them alone, they'll reduce your pest population for free. They're year-round residents but most visible when insect activity peaks, the warmer months.

What you can do yourself

Huntsman spiders don't require chemical treatment. If you want to discourage them, focus on reducing the insects they hunt:

  • Reduce outdoor lighting at night. Insects are attracted to light; fewer insects means fewer huntsman. If you must use outdoor lights, choose warm-toned bulbs (insects respond less to red wavelengths).
  • Minimise harbourage (clutter). Clear boxes away from walls, don't leave piles of clothes or papers on floors, store firewood away from the house.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and pipes entering the house. Fewer entry points for insects means fewer huntsman.
  • Vacuum regularly to remove egg sacs and web material.

If you find a huntsman inside and want it gone, simply catch it in a glass, slide paper underneath, and release it outside. No spray needed. The label is the law on all pesticide products; read the APVMA PubCRIS database if you're checking product claims.

What not to do about huntsman spiders

Don't spray. Huntsman aren't a pest and pesticides carry unnecessary risk. Over-the-counter sprays aren't selective, they kill everything, and people often apply them off-label in doses they remember wrong.

Huntsman rarely bite and never bite unprovoked. If you've already sprayed your home with a supermarket pesticide, get it checked by a licensed pest professional to make sure the application was applied to label and your home is safe for re-entry. An incorrect spray job is worse than leaving the huntsman alone.

Frequently asked

Do huntsman spiders bite?

Huntsman can bite if trapped or handled roughly, but it's exceptionally rare. They're escape-first spiders. If bitten, the venom is too weak to cause more than mild discomfort, like a mosquito bite.

Is huntsman venom poisonous to humans?

No. Venom is injected; poison is ingested. Huntsman have venom designed for insects, not humans. It poses no medical risk to people.

Are huntsman spiders dangerous in Perth?

No. Huntsman are harmless to people and beneficial to your home. The spiders that need managing are redbacks and white-tails. Huntsman eat pest insects like cockroaches and flies.

Should I kill a huntsman spider I find in my home?

No need to. Catch it in a glass, slide paper underneath, and release it outside. It will hunt pest insects for free. If you're uncomfortable handling it, simply leave it, it won't bite you.

Where do huntsman spiders hide in Perth homes?

In roof voids, behind curtains, under furniture, in sheds and garages, around windows where insects gather, and in cavities behind walls. They follow insect prey, so any area with flying insects attracts them.

Do I need pest control for huntsman spiders?

No. Huntsman don't require professional treatment. They're beneficial. If you want to discourage them, reduce clutter (harbourage), use warm outdoor lighting, and seal entry gaps.

How long do huntsman spiders live?

Two to three years typically. They're longer-lived than many other spiders, which is why you might see the same huntsman in your home for several seasons.

Are huntsman spiders active all year in Perth?

Yes, but they're most visible in spring and summer when insect activity peaks and temperatures are higher. In cooler months they shelter in wall cavities and roof voids but remain present.
