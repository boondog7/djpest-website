What is a huntsman spider?

Huntsman spiders belong to the family Sparassidae. They are large, flat spiders with long legs that extend outward and forward, giving them a wide stance that can spread to 15 centimetres or more. The colour ranges from brown to grey to green, depending on the species. Most huntsman spiders in Perth are brown or tan.

The key difference between a huntsman and a web-building spider is posture and hunting method. Huntsmen sit flat and exposed, legs spread wide. They are active hunters that catch insects on the move, not web-builders that wait for prey. This body shape is an adaptation for speed and agility when stalking.

Are huntsman spiders venomous?

Yes, huntsman spiders are venomous. They inject venom into their prey, usually insects and small arthropods, to paralyse them before eating. However, their venom is not a health threat to humans. It is designed to work on small invertebrates, not on mammals our size.

Bites to humans are extremely rare. Huntsmen have no reason to bite unless crushed or feeling threatened. Even if bitten, the venom would cause at most minor local effects like slight swelling or irritation. Medical attention is not needed.

Where huntsman spiders hide in Perth homes

Huntsmen favour spaces where they can hunt actively. In Perth homes, this means:

  • Roof voids, where flying insects congregate.
  • Sub-floors in homes with raised timber floors, common in northern suburbs older properties.
  • Behind curtains, picture frames, and shelves.
  • Garages and sheds.
  • Under bark or in branches near external lighting.

They are not drawn to food or rubbish like cockroaches are. They are not attracted to moisture like silverfish. They live where prey is available. If your home has few flying insects, you will see fewer huntsmen.

Why huntsman spiders appear in spring

In spring (September to November in Perth), temperatures rise and insects become more active. Huntsmen follow their prey. This is when homeowners notice them most, because both spiders and their food are more visible. On warmer evenings, huntsmen are more likely to move about hunting. They may enter homes through open windows and doors, following insects attracted to lights.

Northern suburbs of Perth, where properties are often brick veneer or raised timber with accessible roof voids, provide ideal hunting grounds. These houses offer shelter and diverse prey. Huntsmen may also enter during the transition from spring to summer as they seek cooler, darker spaces during the hottest part of the day.

What you can do if you find a huntsman

The simplest approach is to catch the spider and release it outside. Use a cup and a piece of paper to trap it gently against a wall or surface. Slide the paper underneath and carry it outside. This takes less than a minute and causes no harm to anyone.

To reduce huntsman sightings in your home:

  • Turn off outdoor lights when not in use, or switch to yellow bulbs that attract fewer insects.
  • Seal large gaps around windows and doors to reduce insects entering.
  • Keep roof voids and sub-floors clear of clutter where prey insects can hide.
  • Remove dead insects from windowsills regularly.
  • Keep external areas tidy and well-maintained to reduce overall pest habitat.

These steps reduce the prey base, which in turn reduces huntsman numbers in your home. If you are concerned about spider populations in general and wish to discuss monitoring or other options, we can help.

What not to do

Do not spray a huntsman with insecticide spray from a supermarket or hardware store. These products are often broad-spectrum and may not be applied to label in a home setting. There is no reason to kill a harmless spider. If you have already sprayed and are concerned about residues, get the area checked by a professional.

Do not crush the spider out of panic. A dead spider on the wall is harder to clean up than a live one caught in a cup. Do not assume the spider will bite. Huntsmen are not aggressive.

Frequently asked

Can a huntsman spider kill a human?

No. Huntsman venom is not lethal to humans. No deaths or serious poisonings from huntsman bites are recorded in Australia. The risk of a bite at all is extremely low.

Do huntsman spiders eat other spiders?

Yes. Huntsmen are aggressive predators that will eat other spiders, insects, and small arthropods. If you have both huntsmen and web-building spiders in your home, the huntsmen may reduce the population of web-builders over time.

Are huntsman spiders found in all Perth suburbs?

Huntsman spiders are found throughout Perth and Australia. They are more common in warmer months and in homes where flying insects are available. Older properties with roof voids and sub-floors, common in the northern suburbs, may see them more often.

What is the difference between a huntsman and a redback spider?

Huntsmen are large, flat, fast-moving spiders that actively hunt. Redbacks are smaller, with a shiny black body and a red hourglass mark on the abdomen, and they build sticky webs. Redbacks can cause medical effects with their bite. Huntsmen cannot. Do not confuse the two.

Do huntsman spiders make webs?

No. Huntsmen do not build webs. They actively hunt prey by sight and speed. This is the main difference between them and web-building spiders. If you see a large web with a big spider on or near it, the spider is likely a huntsman resting, not a web-builder.

Should I be afraid of huntsman spiders?

There is no reason to be afraid of a huntsman spider. They do not bite unless crushed, they do not seek out humans, and their venom is not a health threat. Many people in Perth choose to leave them alone and even value them for eating insects around the home.

How long do huntsman spiders live?

Most huntsman species live for around one to two years as adults. Females may live longer than males. After mating, females lay eggs in a silk egg sac and guard it until spiderlings hatch. Huntsmen are solitary except during mating and egg care.
