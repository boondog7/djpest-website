How to get rid of cockroaches (2026 Australian guide).

You saw a cockroach. You're now Googling at 11pm. Welcome — you're not alone, and the answer isn't "spray everywhere and pray". Here's what actually works, what doesn't, and when DIY is fine vs when you need a licensed operator. Written by a Perth pest controller, no fluff.

Published 1 May 2026 · Reading time: 9 minutes · By DJ Pest, licensed PMT, Perth WA

Step one: figure out which cockroach you've got.

There are about 6 cockroach species you'll encounter in an Australian home, but three of them cause 95% of the calls — and the treatment for each is meaningfully different. Spraying the wrong product at the wrong species is a great way to wonder why your roach problem isn't going away.

German cockroach (Blattella germanica)

Small (12-15mm), tan/light brown with two dark stripes running down the back. Lives indoors in warm, humid voids — kitchens, bathrooms, behind fridges, inside microwaves, under dishwashers. This is the one you have to take seriously. They breed fast (one female = 30-40 babies per egg case, 4-8 cases over a 200-day life), they hide in voids you can't reach, and they develop chemical resistance quickly. If you've sprayed and they keep coming back, this is probably your species.

American cockroach (Periplaneta americana)

Large (35-40mm), reddish-brown, fast-moving, sometimes flies. Lives in drains, sub-floors, sewer systems, and outside in mulch beds. They come up through the floor wastes at night looking for water and food. Less of a breeding-population problem than Germans — usually individual incursions from a sewer or sub-floor source. Treatment focus is the source, not the kitchen.

Australian cockroach (Periplaneta australasiae)

Similar size to American but with distinctive yellow markings on the wings and pronotum. Outdoor preferring — wood piles, garden mulch, garage corners. Less commonly an indoor breeding problem; usually they wander in and you spot one on the way to the kitchen. External harbourage clean-up + perimeter spray solves this one.

"If you don't ID the species first, you're throwing money at the wrong product."

Why "spray and forget" doesn't work.

Here's the problem with reaching for a can of Mortein or any aerosol spray: it kills 5% of your cockroach population — the adults that are out walking around when you spray. The other 95% are inside wall voids, behind appliances, inside hatching egg cases (oothecae), and in places aerosols don't reach. They breed back faster than the survivors die.

German cockroaches in particular have what entomologists call insecticide resistance. Sites that have been hit with the same active ingredient (typically pyrethroids — bifenthrin, permethrin) repeatedly often have populations that genuinely no longer respond to it. Modern professional treatment rotates active ingredients and combines them with insect growth regulators (IGRs) — chemicals that prevent juveniles from maturing into reproductive adults. That breaks the cycle even if some adults survive.

What actually works (the 7-step protocol).

  1. ID the species. See above. Don't skip this.
  2. Place 3-5 sticky monitor traps in corners of kitchen, behind fridge, under sink, in pantry, and bathroom. Leave them 3 days. The catch tells you where the harbourage zones are and roughly how many you're dealing with.
  3. Apply gel bait inside cabinetry voids and behind appliances. Hydrazide-based gel (the active ingredient is hydramethylnon, fipronil, or imidacloprid) placed as 3-5mm dots. NEVER on food-prep surfaces. Available at hardware stores and via vet/pest supply shops. Replace every 2-4 weeks until traps are catching nothing.
  4. Add IGR (insect growth regulator). Pyriproxyfen or hydroprene in liquid or aerosol. Apply along wall/floor junctions of kitchen and bathroom per label directions. Keeps working 90+ days.
  5. Fix the why. Repair leaking taps, store food in sealed containers, take out rubbish daily, vacuum behind appliances weekly. Cockroaches need food and water. Remove either and you cripple them.
  6. Seal entry points. Caulk gaps around plumbing, pipe penetrations, weep holes wider than 6mm, gaps between cabinetry and wall. Pay particular attention to where the dishwasher hose enters cabinetry, sink waste pipes, and around the back of the fridge.
  7. Re-monitor at 14 days. Re-deploy the sticky traps. Catch should be down 80%+ from baseline. If not, you've missed a harbourage zone — reset and find it (usually inside an appliance you didn't pull out).

What doesn't work (don't waste your money).

  • Boric acid alone. It works on workers that walk through it, but cockroaches groom themselves obsessively and will avoid heavy dustings. Useful as part of a multi-tool approach, useless on its own.
  • Aerosol "kill on contact" sprays. See above — kills 5%, scatters the rest deeper into voids.
  • Bay leaves, cucumber peel, peppermint oil. All popular on TikTok. None of them have any meaningful effect on a real infestation. They might temporarily mask the chemical trail roaches use to find food, but the population doesn't decline.
  • Bug bombs / foggers. Push roaches into wall voids where they breed back. Considered actively counterproductive by most professional pest controllers.
  • Ultrasonic repellers. No scientific evidence they work. Multiple consumer-test studies have shown zero effect on cockroach populations. Save your money.

When DIY is fine, and when you need a pro.

DIY is fine when:

  • You've spotted 1-3 cockroaches over a few weeks and sticky traps are catching less than 5 over 3 days.
  • It's an American or Australian (large outdoor type) — you mostly need to fix the entry point and treat the source.
  • You're in a stand-alone house with no shared walls.
  • You're catching them, but it's manageable and you're seeing decline.

Call a licensed pest controller when:

  • You're seeing cockroaches during the day. (Cockroaches are nocturnal — daytime sightings mean the population is so large it's spilling out of harbourage at night.)
  • You're in an apartment or townhouse — German cockroaches travel between units through wall cavities, and uncoordinated treatment usually fails.
  • You've treated multiple times yourself and they keep returning.
  • You run a commercial kitchen, café, food premises — there are regulatory implications for an active infestation.
  • You have very young children or immunocompromised family members — cockroach allergens are documented asthma triggers.

Common questions Perth homeowners ask.

Are cockroaches dangerous?

They don't bite. They are, however, mechanical vectors of Salmonella, E. coli, and Staphylococcus. They walk through drains and refuse, then walk across food prep surfaces. Their droppings and shed skins are documented asthma and allergy triggers, particularly in kids. The food safety implications are why commercial kitchens have regulatory pressure to maintain ongoing pest programs.

Why do I see them in a "clean" house?

Cleanliness reduces opportunity, but a single dripping tap and an apartment block neighbour with an active population is enough. Don't beat yourself up — German cockroaches are a coordination problem more than a cleanliness problem.

Will one treatment fix it forever?

For most small-to-medium infestations, one professional treatment + IGR + your follow-up hygiene is enough to clear the active population for 6-12 months. For apartments or chronic-pressure properties (food premises, old plumbing), an ongoing 3-monthly program is realistic. Anyone promising "100% gone forever" from a single visit is selling, not treating.

Are gel baits safe around pets and kids?

When applied per label, yes. The dose toxic to cockroaches is well below the dose problematic to a 10kg dog or 15kg child, and gels are placed inside cabinetry voids where pets and kids can't reach. We brief on placements as part of any professional job.
