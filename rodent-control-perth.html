Why rodents are different in Perth.

Three rodent species cause 99% of Perth pest calls — and they each behave differently, so the treatment differs. Black rats (Rattus rattus) are climbers; they get into roof voids via gutter joints and eaves. They're our most common Northern Suburbs job. Norway rats (Rattus norvegicus) are larger, ground-based, and prefer sub-floors, sewers, and burrow entries. Less common in Perth metro, more common in commercial premises and older inner-suburb properties. House mice (Mus musculus) are everywhere — they don't need much, just a 6mm gap and access to dropped food.

A rat can squeeze through a gap the size of a 20c coin. A mouse can squeeze through a gap the width of a Bic biro. Rodents don't enter homes for shelter when shelter is everywhere — they enter for food and water. Fixing a single dripping tap can do more than three rounds of bait. We always look at why before we treat what.

How we treat rodents (properly).

1. Identify the species before we touch a bait station

Black rat, Norway rat, or mouse — the trap, bait, and placement differ. Black rats need elevated stations or roof void placements. Norway rats need ground-level burrow runs. Mice need close-spaced stations (every 2-3 metres) at every wall corner. Generic "set and forget" baiting wastes bait and gives you a false-negative result.

2. Map every entry point ≥ 6mm

The treatment doesn't end the problem — sealing does. Common entry points we map: gutter joints, eaves, unsealed roof penetrations (vents, plumbing stacks, antenna mounts), sub-floor vents, weep holes wider than 10mm, garage door seals, and gaps where conduits enter the slab.

3. Tamper-resistant external bait + internal trapping

We default to external bait stations (locked, anchored, child- and pet-safe) for the perimeter. Internal placements switch to snap traps or A24 self-resetting traps — bait inside the home introduces secondary poisoning risk for pets that catch a sick rodent. Active ingredients are second-generation anticoagulants (bromadiolone, brodifacoum) — single-feed effective, label-rate dosed, residue-tracked.

4. The 14-day follow-up

Rats are neophobic — they avoid new objects in their territory for 3-7 days. So a station that looks "untouched" at day 5 isn't a failure, it's normal. We come back at 14 days, check consumption, reposition stations along the runway, and confirm clearance. Mice are less cautious — they typically feed within 24-48 hours.

Frequently asked about rodents.

How do I tell if I have rats or mice?

Rats are larger (15-25cm body, dark brown to black), leave 8-12mm droppings, gnaw holes the size of a 50c coin, and run heavy across roof voids. Mice are smaller (6-10cm, lighter grey/brown), leave 3-6mm droppings the size of rice grains, and gnaw smaller holes (10-15mm). Treatment differs — rats need bigger bait stations and stronger baits.

How much does rodent control cost in Perth?

From $180 for a standard treatment — external tamper-resistant bait stations, entry-point assessment, and a 14-day follow-up. Larger or sub-floor jobs from $260. Fixed price quoted before we start.

Are rodent baits safe around pets and kids?

Yes when applied properly. We use tamper-resistant external bait stations — locked, anchored, accessible only by rodents. Internal placements switch to snap traps or live traps where pets and kids are present. We brief you on what to watch for and provide a re-entry timeline.

How long until the rats are gone?

Most domestic infestations clear within 14-21 days. Rats are neophobic — they avoid new objects in their territory for 3-7 days before they'll feed on a bait station. We schedule a 14-day follow-up to check consumption and reposition. Mice are less neophobic — they typically feed within 24-48 hours.

Will they come back?

Re-infestation is almost always due to unsealed entry points. We map every gap larger than 6mm (mice) or 12mm (rats), seal them with rodent-proof materials, and our 3-month warranty covers re-treatment if rodents return. After 12 months, an annual top-up is worth considering — especially in bushland-adjacent suburbs.

Do you treat the roof void as well as outside?

Yes. Most Perth Northern Suburbs rat infestations are roof void activity (Rattus rattus, the black rat — a climber). We physically access the void where safe to do so, identify entry points (typically gutter joints, eave gaps, unsealed roof penetrations), bait or trap internally, then seal externally.

What's the difference between black rats and Norway rats?

Black rats (Rattus rattus) climb — they're roof void specialists, common across Perth's Northern Suburbs. Norway rats (Rattus norvegicus) are ground/burrow rats — they prefer sewers, sub-floors, and ground-level entry. We identify the species first because the trap and bait placement differs significantly.

Can I just use shop-bought baits?

You can — but supermarket baits are generally first-generation anticoagulants (warfarin, coumatetralyl) which require multiple feedings to be lethal. Modern professional baits (bromadiolone, brodifacoum) are second-generation, single-feed effective. The bigger issue isn't the bait — it's tamper-resistance and placement strategy. A properly placed station against a wall along the rodent runway works; a tossed-on-the-ground bait block does not.
