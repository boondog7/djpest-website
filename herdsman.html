Why Herdsman gets the pests it gets

Herdsman Lake was gazetted as a suburban area in 1928 and the ground around it was swamp and market garden until the northern side was dredged and filled in the 1980s to make way for Jon Sanders Drive and the office and showroom precinct around Walters Drive. That gives the suburb two kinds of building: modern slab-on-ground offices, warehouses and showrooms in the business park, and a lakeside strip of homes and villas on Herdsman Parade and Selby Street ranging from mid-century brick-and-tile to 1980s and 1990s two-storey townhouses. The soil is Bassendean grey sand with peat and a high water table near the water, so subterranean termites have moisture all year and treated zones need to be installed with the water table in mind.

The lake itself is the story. Roughly 300 hectares of open water, reed beds and paperbark fringe, it breeds mosquitoes and midges from spring into autumn and holds a year-round population of rats that forage along Pearson Street, Selby Street and the walking loop and into roof spaces and office kitchens. Black house spiders and orb weavers thrive on the insect supply around every lit window and carpark light, and American cockroaches move up from the stormwater drains that feed the lake. We treat the offices with lunchroom cockroach programs and external rodent stations on a plan, and the lakeside homes with roof-void and perimeter work timed around the mosquito season rather than the calendar.

What's active now

This season in Herdsman.

Spring at Herdsman Lake: the first warm evenings bring mosquitoes and midges off the reed beds to the balconies on Herdsman Parade and the outdoor break areas in the business park. Black house spiders build up on lit walls, ants move into office kitchens, and rats that wintered in the roof head back to the lake edge. Older lakeside homes with timber roof framing should have an AS 4349.3 termite inspection booked before the November swarm season.

Sep – NovSpring: ants and spiders wake up

Warmer soil brings coastal brown ants and redbacks out. Good time for a perimeter treatment before summer.

Nov – AprSummer: termite swarm season

Subterranean termites swarm on warm, humid evenings. Sandy northern-suburb soils and garden timber raise the risk.

Mar – MayAutumn: cockroaches move indoors

German and Australian cockroaches follow warmth and food into kitchens as nights cool.

May – AugWinter: rodents in the roof

Rats and mice look for warm voids. Sealing entry points now beats baiting later.

Questions

Asked in Herdsman.

Can you do anything about mosquitoes from Herdsman Lake?

Nobody can treat the lake, but the property can be managed. We treat the shaded resting harbourage around the house or office, advise on screens, drains and standing water, and time visits to the start of the season rather than after it peaks. Products are APVMA-registered and applied to label. Read more.

Are the homes on Herdsman Parade at termite risk?

Yes, more than most. The ground beside the lake is wet grey sand and peat that gives subterranean termites moisture all year, and the older brick-and-tile homes have timber roof framing. An annual inspection to AS 4349.3 is the sensible baseline, and where a management system is warranted it is installed to AS 3660.2 with the water table taken into account.

Do you treat ants in the Herdsman Business Park offices?

Yes. Coastal brown ants nest under carparks and paving on the drier ground along Jon Sanders Drive and Walters Drive and trail into office kitchens. Repellent sprays split the colony; we use non-repellent products and baits so the treatment reaches the nest, and we document the visit for the property manager. Ant management.

Why are there so many rats near the lake?

Herdsman Lake's reed beds, drains and the walking loop's bins support a year-round rodent population. Our approach for lakeside homes is roof-void baiting plus proofing at eaves and pipe penetrations, and for the offices a numbered external station program with a service log.
