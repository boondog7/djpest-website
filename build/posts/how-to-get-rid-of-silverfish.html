What silverfish are and why they're in your house

Silverfish are small, wingless insects about 10 to 15 mm long, with a flattened, carrot-shaped body that tapers to three long bristles at the tail. They're covered in fine silvery or grey scales and move with a quick, fish-like wriggle, which is where the name comes from. In Australian homes you'll mostly see the common silverfish (Lepisma saccharinum) and the larger grey or long-tailed silverfish (Ctenolepisma species), which copes with drier conditions.

They're after three things: moisture, starch and somewhere dark to hide. Starch means paper, book bindings, wallpaper paste, cardboard, cereal, flour, dried pet food and even the sizing in some fabrics. A Perth home usually gives them all three in the bathroom, the laundry, the kitchen pantry, the linen cupboard and the roof void.

Silverfish live a long time for an insect (often two years or more) and can survive weeks without food. That's why a few leftovers in a cupboard can quietly keep a population going, and why a single spray rarely finishes the job.

Signs you have a silverfish problem

Because they're nocturnal, the first sign is often a silverfish in the bath or sink in the morning. They fall in chasing moisture and can't climb the smooth sides back out. Other clues:

  • Irregular holes or scraped patches on paper, book spines, photos, cardboard boxes or wallpaper
  • Yellowish stains or tiny pepper-like droppings on shelves and paper
  • Shed skins (they keep moulting as adults) in cupboards and drawers
  • Small holes along the folds of stored clothes, linen or curtains
  • Silverfish darting away when you move boxes in the garage, shed or storeroom

How to get rid of silverfish: the step-by-step

Silverfish management is mostly about taking away what they need. Do these in order and give it three to four weeks before judging the result.

  1. Dry the place out. Run the bathroom and laundry exhaust fans during and after every shower and wash, fix dripping taps and leaking traps under sinks, and keep wet towels off the floor. A cheap hygrometer will tell you whether a room sits above about 60% humidity, which is where silverfish thrive. A dehumidifier in a stuffy wardrobe or storeroom makes a real difference.
  2. Clear out the paper. Old newspapers, magazines, cardboard boxes and junk mail are food and shelter in one. Recycle what you don't need and move keepers into sealed plastic tubs.
  3. Seal the pantry. Put flour, cereal, rice, pasta and pet food into airtight containers. Wipe up crumbs on shelves and vacuum the corners.
  4. Vacuum hiding spots. Skirting boards, the backs of cupboards, under the bed, bookshelves and wardrobe floors. Vacuuming picks up eggs, droppings and scales. Empty the bag or canister outside.
  5. Close the gaps. Fill cracks along skirting boards, around pipes under sinks and where bench tops meet walls with a flexible sealant. This removes the daytime hiding places they rely on.
  6. Trap and monitor. Sticky traps placed along skirting boards and inside cupboards show you where the activity is and whether numbers are falling. Check and replace them weekly.
  7. Use a registered product where needed. If numbers stay high, a household insect product that lists silverfish on its label can be used in cracks and crevices. Only use APVMA-registered products, applied to the label, and keep them out of reach of kids and pets.

Why silverfish keep coming back

If you've cleaned up and they're still turning up, look for one of these:

  • A hidden moisture source. A slow leak behind the shower wall, a damp subfloor, a blocked gutter feeding water into a wall cavity or poor roof ventilation.
  • A reservoir in the roof void or wall cavities. Insulation, stored boxes and dust in the roof can hold a large population that drops into rooms through cornices, light fittings and exhaust fans.
  • Stored items in the garage or shed. Cardboard boxes of old paperwork or clothes can keep feeding them and reinfest the house.
  • Gaps around the home's edge. Grey silverfish often live outside under mulch, pavers and leaf litter, then move in through weep holes and gaps under doors.

DIY or call a technician?

DIY is usually enough when you see the odd silverfish in the bathroom and the damage is minor. It's worth getting a licensed technician in when:

  • You're seeing them every night in several rooms, even after a month of drying out and decluttering
  • There's damage to things you can't replace: books, documents, photos, artwork or clothing
  • You suspect they're living in the roof void, which isn't a job for a ladder and a spray can
  • You have other pests at the same time (cockroaches, spiders, ants) and want one visit to cover them

A professional treatment focuses on the places silverfish actually live: a dust into the roof void and wall voids where it's accessible, a crack-and-crevice treatment in wet areas and cupboards, and an external band around the home's perimeter. Frequently asked

Are silverfish harmful to people?

No. Silverfish don't bite or sting and aren't known to spread disease. Their scales and droppings can add to household dust, which may bother people with dust allergies, but the main problem is damage to paper, fabric and stored food.

What attracts silverfish to a house?

Humidity, starchy food and dark hiding places. Bathrooms, laundries, pantries, bookshelves, linen cupboards and roof voids are the usual spots.

Does one silverfish mean an infestation?

Not necessarily. One in the bath now and then is common in Perth homes, especially near bathrooms. Several a week, in several rooms, or fresh damage to paper and fabric suggests an established population.

How long does it take to get rid of silverfish?

With moisture fixed and food removed, most homes see numbers drop within three to six weeks. Because silverfish are long-lived and eggs hatch over several weeks, it takes patience and ongoing monitoring with sticky traps.

Can silverfish live in the roof?

Yes. Roof voids with insulation, dust and stored boxes can hold large numbers, especially where ventilation is poor. If they keep appearing near cornices, downlights or exhaust fans, the roof is a likely source.

Do silverfish eat clothes?

They can. They go for starch and sizing in fabrics and for food or sweat stains, so stored clothing, linen and curtains can end up with small holes or yellow marks.

Will a general pest treatment cover silverfish?

Yes. At DJ Pest, a general pest treatment covers silverfish along with cockroaches, spiders and internal ants, using APVMA-registered products applied to their labels.

Do silverfish mean I have a moisture problem?

Often, yes. A steady silverfish population is a good prompt to check for leaks, poor ventilation and damp spots, which are worth fixing for the house's sake anyway.
