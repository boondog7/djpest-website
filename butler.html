Why Butler gets the pests it gets

Butler is one of the youngest suburbs we service. Development started in 2003 under the Butler-Ridgewood Structure Plan, and the Satterley-built Brighton estate filled it in over the following fifteen years, from Brighton Village at the corner of Marmion Avenue and Kingsbridge Boulevard across to the Mitchell Freeway. The housing is almost entirely double brick on concrete slab with steel or timber roof framing, small lots, paved side access and alfresco areas poured against the slab. The ground is yellow Spearwood sand over Tamala limestone, warm and free-draining, which suits coastal brown ants perfectly. Newer homes have a physical or chemical termite system installed at construction, but slab penetrations, added patios and garden beds built up against the brickwork are where those systems get bridged.

Butler sits about two kilometres from the ocean, close enough for the sea breeze but far enough that the pressure comes from bush and water rather than salt. The Mitchell Freeway forms the eastern boundary with Neerabup bushland beyond it, remnant vegetation is retained in reserves through the estate, and the lake at Kingsbridge Park is a standing-water source in a suburb that otherwise has none. That combination brings rodents and native cockroaches into the back fences, mosquitoes and midges around the lake in the warm months, and a steady supply of termite colonies in the retained bush that new homes have been built beside. We adjust for it: thorough external perimeters, attention to slab edges and penetrations, and rodent stations on the bush-facing sides, with APVMA-registered products applied to label.

Termites

Coastal brown ants

The number one call from Butler. Warm Spearwood sand under paving and small lots that share a colony across four or five properties. Colony-level treatment with slow-acting non-repellent product and bait.

Rats and mice

Bush across the freeway and the reserves through the estate, plus the food precinct around Butler Central and the station. Steel roof framing does not stop rats; unscreened eaves and garage roller-door gaps let them in. Rodent management.

Mosquitoes

The lake at Kingsbridge Park and the retained wetland vegetation nearby breed mosquitoes and midges from spring to autumn. Harbourage treatment of the yard plus screen and drainage advice. Mosquito treatment.

How far is Butler from your base?

About thirty minutes from Warwick up the Mitchell Freeway, exiting at Butler Boulevard or Hester Avenue. Same-day for active pests is usually possible. We schedule Butler alongside Alkimos, Quinns Rocks and Clarkson so a booked visit is normally within a day or two.

My Butler home is only ten years old. Do I really need a termite inspection?

Yes. The builder-installed termite system is only as good as the day it was installed. Once patios, paving and garden beds are built against the brickwork the system is bridged, and Butler homes back onto retained bush where colonies already live. An inspection to AS 4349.3 every twelve months keeps the manufacturer warranty on many systems valid. Book an inspection.

Why are coastal brown ants so bad in Butler?

Warm, free-draining Spearwood sand under paving and small lots is exactly what they like, and one super-colony can span four or five properties. Repellent sprays split the colony and move it. We use slow-acting non-repellent products and baits, APVMA-registered and applied to label, that the workers carry back to the queens. Read more.

We get mosquitoes near Kingsbridge Park. Can you help?

We can reduce them. The lake and its vegetation are the breeding source and cannot be treated by us, but we treat the resting harbourage in your yard, shrubs, fence lines and under the alfresco, with an APVMA-registered product applied to label, and we point out the gutters, pot saucers and drains on your side that breed them.
