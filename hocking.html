Why Hocking gets the pests it gets

Hocking was farmland and market gardens until the 1990s, named for a Wanneroo Road Board chairman from the early 1900s. The housing is almost all slab-on-ground brick and tile from the last twenty years, much of it in the Belvedere estate, with newer stages still under construction on the eastern side. Slab homes keep the frame off the ground, but termites enter through slab penetrations, expansion joints and along the slab edge wherever a garden bed, lawn or paving has been built up over the weep holes. The ground is the same Spearwood yellow sand that grew Wanneroo's vegetables, free-draining and warm, which is why coastal brown ants have colonised the paving in every stage of the estate.

What makes Hocking different from an older suburb is the edge. Remnant market gardens, vacant lots under development and the bush and wetlands of Yellagonga Regional Park across Wanneroo Road all sit within a few hundred metres of new homes. Cleared land pushes rodents, spiders and native cockroaches into the nearest finished street, and the wetlands send mosquitoes over on spring evenings. Gungurru, Chesterfield, Amery and Hinckley Parks add irrigated turf and fresh mulch that ants and spiders love. We treat Hocking with those edges in mind and pay close attention to the termite management system the builder installed, because that is the home's first line of defence.

What we treat most in Hocking

The Hocking pest short-list.

Termites

Slab penetrations, built-up garden beds over the slab edge and construction pressure from neighbouring lots. Inspections to AS 4349.3, checking the builder's system and topping it up to AS 3660.2 where needed, with an APVMA-registered product applied to its label. Termite inspection.

Coastal brown ants

Under driveways, paving and alfresco slabs across the estate. Non-repellent products and baits applied to label to collapse the colony rather than scatter it. Ant management.

Rats and mice

Displaced from cleared lots and the remaining market gardens, and drawn to sheds, chook pens and roof voids. Baiting in lockable stations plus proofing. Rodent management.

Spiders and cockroaches

Redbacks in retic boxes and under alfresco furniture, white-tails in fresh mulch, and native bush cockroaches wandering in from the paddocks. Low-odour non-staining products indoors, applied to label. Spider management.

Questions

Asked in Hocking.

How long from Warwick to Hocking?

About twelve minutes up Wanneroo Road. Same-day for active pests is usually possible.

My Hocking home is new. Do termites really get into slab homes?

Yes. Termites enter slab homes through penetrations for plumbing and services, expansion joints and along the outside of the slab where soil, mulch or paving has been built up above the weep holes. Your builder's termite management system also needs checking and maintaining. An inspection to AS 4349.3 confirms it is intact and documents the home for resale. Book an inspection.

Why does every house in our Hocking street have ants?

Because the estate sits on warm yellow sand, coastal brown ant habitat, and the colony spans several properties under the paving. A repellent spray at one house just moves the workers next door. We use non-repellent products and baits applied to label so the treatment reaches the queens, and a combined treatment across neighbours works best. Read more.

Are the vacant lots and market gardens in Hocking a pest problem?

They can be. Land clearing and earthworks displace rodents, spiders and native cockroaches into the nearest finished homes, and gardens under cultivation hold moisture and food. If a lot near you is being developed, an external treatment and a check of your roofline and shed before the earthworks start heads most of it off.
