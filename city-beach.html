Why City Beach gets the pests it gets

City Beach was laid out by the Perth Road Board on garden-city lines after it bought the old Lime Kilns Estate in 1917, but most of the housing came later. The central section around The Boulevard and Empire Village was built as the athletes' village for the 1962 British Empire and Commonwealth Games, and those low brick homes on generous blocks are now being replaced one by one with big two-storey rebuilds. The ocean streets sit on young white Quindalup dune sand; behind them the ground rises onto Tamala limestone, the same calcareous rock that was quarried in Bold Park for early Perth. Limestone close to the surface means termite management systems here are usually drilled and injected rather than trenched, and the rock holds moisture pockets that termites use in summer.

The pest pressure comes from the bush. Bold Park, at 437 hectares, is the largest remnant bushland in the inner suburbs and it runs straight into the back fences of the southern section, with Perry Lakes Reserve and its wetland just to the east. That bush carries native cockroaches, huntsmen, redbacks, rodents and the occasional dugite, and they all move onto the adjoining properties in spring. Salt air off the beach corrodes the mesh and flashings that keep rats out of roofs along West Coast Highway and Oceanic Drive, and the foreshore cafes and Ocean Village shops add a steady food supply. We treat City Beach with a bush edge in mind: proofing, harbourage reduction and products applied to label.

Does living next to Bold Park change how you treat a City Beach home?

Yes. Bush-edge homes get more huntsmen, native cockroaches, rodents and redbacks than a home two streets in, and the pressure never really stops. We concentrate on the fence line, the roofline and the harbourage close to the house, treat to label, and recommend proofing so the bush stays in the bush. Spider treatment.

Are the original 1962 Games village homes a termite risk?

They are now sixty years old, with timber roof framing and no modern chemical barrier, and many have had extensions and paving that bridge whatever protection existed. Where limestone is close to the surface we drill and inject a system to AS 3660.2 rather than trench. Start with an inspection to AS 4349.3. Book an inspection.

Why are coastal brown ants so bad in City Beach?

Warm, well-drained dune sand over limestone is the habitat they were named for. They form linked super-colonies with many queens across several properties. Repellent sprays split the colony; slow-acting non-repellent products and baits, applied to label, collapse it. Read more.
