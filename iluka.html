Why Iluka gets the pests it gets

Iluka was developed through the 1990s and 2000s between Burns Beach Road, Shenton Avenue and the ocean, with O'Mara Boulevard as its spine. The homes are large slab-on-ground builds, many two-storey, with rendered walls, steel or treated-pine roof framing, alfresco areas and paving to every boundary. The ground is Quindalup coastal sand along the foreshore streets and shallow yellow sand over Tamala limestone further inland, which is why so much of the suburb is built on limestone retaining walls. Every home carried a termite management system to AS 3660.2 from construction, but retaining walls and paving laid against the brickwork bridge it, and limestone close to the surface means any replacement system is drilled and injected rather than trenched.

The Iluka Foreshore Reserve runs the length of the suburb's coast, a strip of consolidated limestone dune and coastal heath between Burns Beach and the Ocean Reef marina. That bush is the source of redbacks, black house spiders and native cockroaches, and the lake in Sir James McCusker Park off Moonflight Crescent adds mosquitoes on still evenings. Salt air corrodes the galvanised mesh and flashings that keep rodents out of roof spaces, so proofing here needs stainless or aluminium. The sea breeze pushes termite alates and flying ants inland on November nights. We treat the ocean-facing and park-facing sides of a home more heavily and use non-repellent products on the coastal brown ant colonies under the paving.

What we treat most in Iluka

The Iluka pest short-list.

Termites

Bridged slab edges, limestone pockets that hold summer moisture and alates blown in from the foreshore reserve. Inspections to AS 4349.3 and drill-and-inject systems to AS 3660.2. Termite inspections.

Coastal brown ants

The name says it. Warm, free-draining sand over limestone with paving and retic everywhere is the habitat coastal brown ants were named for. Colony-level treatment with a slow-acting non-repellent product, never a repellent spray.

Rats in the roof

The foreshore bush and the sports complex reserves keep the population up, and salt-corroded flashings give them a way in. Rodent work in Iluka is a proofing job as much as a baiting one. Rodent management.

Spiders and cockroaches

Redbacks under limestone coping and in retic boxes, black house spiders on rendered walls, native cockroaches from the dunes and American cockroaches out of the stormwater lines. Spider treatment and general pest treatment.

Questions

Asked in Iluka.

Does salt air change how you treat an Iluka home?

It changes the proofing advice. Galvanised mesh and flashings corrode faster near the ocean, so gaps at eaves and roof penetrations open sooner and rodents find them. We recommend stainless or aluminium mesh for sealing and we check the roofline on the ocean-facing side more carefully than we would inland. Rodent management.

My Iluka home was built in the early 2000s. Is termite risk real on a slab?

Yes. The system installed at construction relied on a visible slab edge, and most Iluka homes have since had limestone retaining walls, garden beds and paving built against the brickwork. Termites use that cover. An inspection to AS 4349.3 finds any bridge and tells you whether the system needs reinstating to AS 3660.2. Book an inspection.

Why are coastal brown ants so bad in Iluka?

Warm, well-drained sand over limestone is the habitat they were named for. They form linked super-colonies with many queens across several properties. Repellent sprays split the colony; slow-acting non-repellent products and baits, APVMA-registered and applied to label, collapse it. Read more.

Do the foreshore reserve and the McCusker Park lake bring more pests?

They do. The coastal heath along the Iluka Foreshore Reserve is a year-round source of redbacks, black house spiders and native cockroaches, and the lake breeds mosquitoes from October. We treat the reserve-facing walls, fence lines and eaves more thoroughly and target the shaded resting spots mosquitoes use around the yard. Mosquito management.
